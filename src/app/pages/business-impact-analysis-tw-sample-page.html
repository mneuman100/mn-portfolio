Provide Company-Wide BCM Training for the BIA

Providing company- wide Business Continuity Management (BCM) training before you start your BIA gives participants and employees a common level of understanding of the process. You should emphasize the objectives and priorities of the BIA and clear up any misunderstandings. Each business unit should come away with a greater awareness of the overall organization and its interdependencies rather than just focusing on their own department.

Another purpose of the training is to get the support and cooperation of your business units. This cooperation will come into play during the interview process where the implementation team and each business unit will work together to document detailed process information.

Training should include:

  1. What is a BIA?

  2. What a BIA isn't

  3. How the BIA fits within the larger BCM plan

  4. How small and large disruptions can affect the whole company

  5. The importance of determining the most critical processes

  6. What is expected of them before, during, and after the BIA

  7. Why a yearly BIA review is vital

Tip:Be sure to stress that the first time around is the hardest. Afterward, the BIA will be a periodic review to document changes in their processes.

Identify Subject Matter Experts

Within each business unit, select the individuals who: (1) do the hands-on job daily (job titles are irrelevant), (2) are the most knowledgeable about each process's workings and dependencies, and (3) know how a disruption in those processes would impact the overall company. These Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) will also provide you with the most accurate critical assessment since they perform these processes regularly.

SMEs are necessary to:

  1. Identify the critical processes within the business unit

  2. Detail the steps needed to complete each critical process

  3. Identify the minimum resources and tools needed to perform each critical process

  4. Help determine the monetary and non-monetary impact to the company if a disruption occurs to the business unit and/or critical process

  5. Help determine the RTO and RPO for the business unit and critical processes

Identify IT Experts

Within the IT department, select the individuals that are the most knowledgeable about: (1) your current IT infrastructure (including physical locations of hardware, vendors used, processes in place, etc.) (2) backup and recovery procedures, (3) the interdependencies within the company, and (4) how disruptions affect the overall company. Since the IT department will ultimately maintain the backup and recovery procedures, having their valuable input in the BIA and BCM is critical to its success. Keep in mind that the IT department also has a vested interest in the BCM's success, since it will affect their budget for years.
